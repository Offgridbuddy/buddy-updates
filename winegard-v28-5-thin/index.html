THE FIVE THIN SPOTS — tap to open
  • 1 (red, right) & 2 (red, left) — the seam tapers to a knife edge: at the front, the triangle trim line crosses each channel wall diagonally, so the join between the sloped top and the wall is not the wall’s full 6.4 mm width. The join first touches at y ≈ −2 at 0.2 mm wide, is 1.6 mm wide at y=0, 3.0 mm at y=2, and only reaches the full 6.4 mm at y ≈ 8. The red ribbons lie on the underside over that stretch of each wall, with a matching patch on the wall’s outer face.
  • 3 (orange, right) & 4 (orange, left) — the wall top stops short: beside the seam, the outer strip of each wall’s top was cut down to a flat ledge at z ≈ −0.7 — about 5–7 mm below the top’s underside — running from the channel’s front face (y=−7.4) to y ≈ 4.5. Forward of the seam (y −7.4 to −3.5) the whole wall top sits at that ledge: the wall does not reach the top at all there.
  • 5 (yellow frame) — the tip tapers to a point: the front tongue is full thickness (9.98 mm) and 20.6 mm wide where it passes the channel’s front face, narrowing to 7.8 mm at the end — and its last ~2 mm round over to a nose: 6.1 mm thick, then 2.6, then ~0.5 mm at the very edge. The yellow frame rings the nose.
  • CHECKED AND CLEAR: the side-wall joins from y ≈ 8 to the rear are the full 6.4 mm everywhere (wider at the gussets); the rear arch meets the underside over 6.5 mm with a 6.5 mm wall — no kiss contact left; the top plate itself measures 9.98 mm thick throughout (the only thinner zone is the bolt-head recess floor at the front hole: 6.8 mm of material above it, by design).
  • ALSO NOTICED (not a join spot): a paper-thin internal sheet, 0.02 mm thick, lies flat at z=0 across much of the channel’s inside and out under both wings (roughly x −30…30, y −7…63) — a leftover face from the boolean builds. At a tenth of a print layer it cannot form in a print, but it can show in viewers as a faint membrane under the part.
